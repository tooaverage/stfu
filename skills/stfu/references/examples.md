# Before and after

Real copy from test pages, rewritten with stfu. Each pair says what changed and why.

## Landing page hero

Before: "Whether you're planning a spring getaway or an autumn adventure, our comprehensive guides leverage monthly weather data to help you seamlessly identify the optimal time to visit. It's that simple."

After: "Find the best month to go. Each guide shows the weather, month by month, for a real city."

Why: it leads with the answer, drops the filler opener, hype words and summary closer, and a 12-month chart does the rest.

## Hero intro

Before: "The short answer is spring (late March to May) or autumn (October to November). The weather is mild, the skies are mostly clear, and you get cherry blossom or autumn leaves. The longer answer depends on where you're going, because Hokkaido and Okinawa have very different seasons from Tokyo and Kyoto."

After: "Go in spring (late March to May) or autumn (October to November). It's mild and mostly clear, with cherry blossom or autumn leaves. Hokkaido and Okinawa have their own seasons."

Why: 51 words became 30, and every fact stayed. "The short answer is" and "The longer answer depends on" were setups.

## Guide intro

Before: "Japan's bullet trains are fast, punctual and surprisingly easy to use once you know the routine. This guide walks first-time visitors through every step, from choosing a ticket to finding your seat."

After: "Book a Shinkansen ticket online, at a ticket machine or at the ticket office. Each way is below, plus seats, luggage and what to do on the day."

Why: the reader came to book a ticket, so that comes first. "This guide walks you through" narrates the page instead of saying something.

## Section intro

Before: "There are three ways to buy a ticket. All of them sell the same seats at the same price, so pick whichever feels most comfortable."

After: "All 3 ways sell the same seats at the same price."

Why: the heading already says there are three ways. The one useful fact stays.

## Warning

Before: "Keep every ticket until you exit. Insert all of them into the gate together. The gate returns any you still need, so take them back. You need them again at your destination."

After: "Keep every ticket until you exit at your destination. The gate gives back the ones you still need."

Why: four sentences said one thing twice. The caveat is shorter but still there.

## Error message

Before: "An unexpected error has occurred while attempting to process your request. Please try again later."

After: "That file's too big. The limit is 25 MB. Try a smaller file, or compress this one."

Why: it says what went wrong and how to fix it, with the real number.

## Empty state

Before: "It looks like you haven't created any projects yet! Get started by clicking the button below to create your very first project."

After: "No projects yet." with a "New project" button.

Why: the button says what to do, so the sentence about the button can go.

## Hype paragraph

Before: "In today's fast-paced world, our robust platform empowers teams to seamlessly streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential."

After: Ask what the product actually does first. With that fact: "Cut your weekly status meeting. Bramble shows what each person shipped, updated every morning."

Why: the before has no facts in it, only hype words. Shorter hype is still hype, so find the fact.

## Form, not just words

Before: twelve month cards, each with a paragraph of weather notes.

After: one table with a row per month: a score badge, the high and low, rain, and a note of about 8 words.

Why: options compared on the same points read faster as a table, and the page gets much shorter without losing a fact.

## Folding detail

Before: children's fares, refunds and IC card limits sat in the middle of the booking steps.

After: the steps stay short, and those details sit under "More ticket details" that the reader can open.

Why: most people only need the steps. The detail is still there for the few who need it.
