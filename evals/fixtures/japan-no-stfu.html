日本Best time to visit Japan

Go late March to May, or November.

Spring brings cherry blossom and dry, mild days. November brings red maples and clear skies. Both are comfortable for long days of walking.

Avoid August to October: heat, humidity and typhoon season

Best time for each city

Japan is 3,000 km long, so the timing shifts. Hokkaido is at its best in summer, when Honshu is too hot. Okinawa is good in early spring and late autumn but sits in the typhoon path from July to September.

Plan around a season

Cherry blossom, autumn leaves, snow and quiet

Blossom moves north through spring and the leaves move south through autumn. Forecasts are published from January (sakura) and September (koyo), so set your exact dates once they are out.

Cherry blossom · 桜

Late March to early April

  1. Late Jan–FebOkinawa
  2. Mar 22–Apr 5Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, Hiroshima
  3. Mid–late AprTohoku (Hirosaki, Kakunodate)
  4. Early MaySapporo and Hakodate

Full bloom lasts about a week. Book a 10-day window in Tokyo or Kyoto and you will almost certainly catch it.

Autumn leaves · 紅葉

Mid-November to early December

  1. Mid–late SepDaisetsuzan, Hokkaido
  2. Mid–late OctNikko, Japanese Alps
  3. Early–mid NovHakone, Mt Fuji lakes
  4. Nov 15–Dec 5Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka

Kyoto temples such as Eikando and Tofuku-ji peak around the last week of November. Go at opening time.

Skiing · スキー

Mid-January to February

  1. Dec–MarSeason open at most resorts
  2. Jan–FebDeepest powder: Niseko, Furano, Rusutsu
  3. Jan–MarHakuba and Nozawa Onsen, 3 hours from Tokyo

Niseko averages around 14 m of snow a season. Book lodging by September for January stays.

Smaller crowds · 静か

Late January, February, early June, early December

  1. Mid Jan–FebColdest but driest; hotel prices drop
  2. May 10–Jun 5After Golden Week, before the rainy season
  3. Dec 1–20Late leaves in Kyoto, winter lights in Tokyo

Lunar New Year (late Jan or Feb) and the Sapporo Snow Festival (early Feb) are busy exceptions.

Dates to work around
  • Golden Week, April 29 to May 5. Japan's biggest holiday. Trains and hotels sell out and prices double.
  • Obon, around August 13 to 16. Families travel home and shinkansen seats go fast.
  • New Year, December 28 to January 3. Many shops and museums close; temples are packed.
Suggested route · 14 days

Tokyo to Osaka in two weeks

Fly into Tokyo and out of Osaka (Kansai airport) so you never backtrack. Every leg is a direct shinkansen ride. Send big bags ahead with a luggage forwarding service (takkyubin) for about ¥2,500 each.

  1. Days 1–4

    Tokyo

    Asakusa and Senso-ji at dawn, Shibuya and Harajuku, teamLab, Shinjuku at night. Day trip to Nikko or Kamakura.

  2. Hikari or Kodama · 35 min · Tokyo → Odawara
  3. Days 5–6

    Hakone

    A night in a ryokan with an onsen. Ride the Hakone loop: mountain railway, ropeway over Owakudani, boat across Lake Ashi. Mt Fuji views on clear mornings.

  4. Hikari · 2 h · Odawara → Kyoto
  5. Days 7–10

    Kyoto

    Fushimi Inari before 8 am, Arashiyama bamboo grove, Gion in the evening, Kiyomizu-dera. Half-day trip to Nara to see Todai-ji and the deer.

  6. Nozomi · 1 h 40 min · Kyoto → Hiroshima
  7. Day 11

    Hiroshima and Miyajima

    Peace Memorial Park and museum, then the ferry to Miyajima. Stay on the island to see the floating torii gate after the day-trippers leave.

  8. Nozomi · 1 h 25 min · Hiroshima → Shin-Osaka
  9. Days 12–14

    Osaka

    Street food in Dotonbori and Kuromon Market, Osaka Castle, Shinsekai. Day 14: the Haruka express reaches Kansai airport in about 50 minutes.

First-timer's guide · 新幹線

How to book a shinkansen ticket

The bullet train is easier than it looks. On the Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka–Hiroshima line there are three kinds of train. All of them use the same tracks and the same tickets; only the number of stops changes.

NozomiFastest. Tokyo–Kyoto in 2 h 15 min. Runs every few minutes.
HikariA few more stops. Tokyo–Kyoto in about 2 h 40 min. Stops at Odawara.
KodamaStops everywhere. Use it only for short hops.

  1. Pick your train and time

    Look up times on Google Maps or Navitime. For most trips, take the next Nozomi. Allow 20 minutes to get through a big station like Tokyo or Shinjuku.

  2. Book online with smartEX

    The smartEX app (by JR Central) sells tickets for Tokyo to Kyoto, Osaka, Hiroshima and Fukuoka in English with a foreign credit card.

    • Bookings open one month ahead at 10 am Japan time.
    • You can change the time for free before departure.
    • For other lines (to Kanazawa, Sendai, Hokkaido), use JR East's online booking site.

  3. Or buy at the station

    Use the red or green ticket machines, which have an English button and take cards and cash. If you need help, go to the ticket office, marked みどりの窓口 (Midori no Madoguchi). Write your destination, date and time on paper to show the staff.

  4. Choose reserved or non-reserved

    A reserved seat costs about ¥500 to ¥900 more and is worth it. Non-reserved cars (usually cars 1–3) are first come, first served.

    • During Golden Week, Obon and New Year, Nozomi trains are reserved-only.
    • Going west from Tokyo, pick seat E (right side, window) to see Mt Fuji about 40 minutes out.

  5. Reserve space for large luggage

    On the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu lines, a bag whose length, width and height add up to more than 160 cm needs a seat with an oversized baggage area. Book it when you buy your ticket. It costs nothing extra, but bringing the bag without a booking costs ¥1,000.

  6. Go through the gate

    You may get two paper tickets, a base fare and an express ticket. Insert them together into the gate and take them back out; you will need them again to exit. With smartEX, tap the IC card (such as Suica) you linked in the app, or scan the QR code.

  7. Find your car and board

    Car numbers are marked on the platform floor and on signs above. Queue in the lane for your car. Trains leave to the second and stop for about a minute, so be at the platform 5 minutes early.

  8. On board

    Eating is fine, so buy an ekiben (station bento) before you board. Turn your seat row around to face friends. Take phone calls in the area between cars.
